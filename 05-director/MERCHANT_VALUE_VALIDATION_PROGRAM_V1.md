# Merchant Value Validation Program — V1.0

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** Director research design — evidence collection not yet performed  
**Purpose:** validate whether the strongest Product-evidence patterns correspond to important merchant problems and usable positioning territory before final Brand Strategy.

## 1. Research boundary

Current canonical evidence establishes what Wossol implements and what competitors publicly claim. It does **not** establish:
- which merchant problems are most painful;
- how frequently those problems occur;
- what merchants currently do instead;
- which problems have meaningful economic/mental/operational consequence;
- which Wossol proof points merchants value;
- what language merchants naturally use;
- whether a candidate territory is distinctive in lived experience;
- willingness to switch, adopt or pay;
- whether merchants trust Wossol's evidence/control model.

No interview, survey, persona or merchant-feedback corpus was found in the Intelligence repository at program start.

Therefore all merchant-value conclusions remain hypotheses until primary research is collected.

## 2. Research priority

### Primary audience
Merchants/operators who personally experience ecommerce operating work across acquisition/store/order/confirmation/delivery/returns/finance/inventory/analytics.

Prioritize diversity of operating model rather than one geography:
- early but active merchants;
- growing merchants with meaningful order volume;
- merchants using multiple tools/providers;
- merchants using managed COD/fulfillment providers;
- merchants with in-house or hybrid operations;
- merchants operating or attempting expansion across more than one market/channel where available.

Do not restrict research to Libya. Libya may be included as one operating context, not the assumed global identity of the brand.

### Secondary roles
Where relevant:
- operations managers;
- confirmation/customer-service operators;
- finance/reconciliation operators;
- performance/growth operators;
- founders/owners who no longer execute every task themselves.

### Shopper/customer research
End-customer/shoppers are **not the first validation audience** for the current brand-positioning question. Run shopper research later when testing customer-facing checkout, confirmation, tracking, trust and communication experiences.

## 3. Core hypotheses to test

### H1 — Reconstruction burden
Merchants repeatedly reconstruct commercial context across tools/providers/workflows, and this creates meaningful time, error, delay or uncertainty.

Product evidence supporting plausibility:
cross-domain identity, Global Search, source/order/shipment/finance joins, historical snapshots.

Validation needed:
frequency, severity, workaround, consequence, willingness to change.

### H2 — Reconciliation burden
Merchants perform meaningful manual reconciliation between operational events and financial/cost evidence.

Validation needed:
what is reconciled, with what tools, how often, by whom, failure modes, economic consequence.

### H3 — Control matters, but control is specific
Merchants value consequential rights such as approve, correct, hold, assign, configure, inspect and override—not generic dashboard visibility.

Validation needed:
which rights matter by workflow; which work merchants prefer to delegate completely.

### H4 — Provenance creates trust only when tied to a decision/problem
Merchants value knowing where a number/status came from, what changed, who acted, and what remains unresolved when that evidence affects money, customer commitments, stock or decisions.

Validation needed:
which provenance matters; where detail becomes noise; what proof is required to trust settlement/profit/performance.

### H5 — Connected context is valuable through work removed, not architecture
“Connected system” matters only if it removes repeated entry, searching, exporting, merging, chasing or explanation.

Validation needed:
merchant language for the problem and proof of consequence.

### H6 — Decision preparation is a real burden
Merchants spend effort preparing evidence before deciding what to do, not merely executing operations.

Validation needed:
recurring decisions, data assembly/calculation, confidence, current advisors/tools, acceptable recommendation authority.

### H7 — Explicit uncertainty can increase trust
For material decisions, merchants may prefer a system that distinguishes known/unknown/partial evidence over confident but opaque answers.

Validation needed:
situations where uncertainty helps vs frustrates; acceptable explanation depth.

### H8 — Managed execution vs merchant agency is segment-dependent
Some merchants prefer providers to remove the work entirely; others need software control because outsourcing hides context or constrains decisions.

Validation needed:
segment boundary and switching triggers.

## 4. Candidate territories to test — not pitches

Do not show these labels at the start of interviews.

Use them only as internal coding hypotheses after unaided problem discovery:

1. **Connected Commercial Truth**
2. **Control without Fragmentation**
3. **Less Work Between the Work**
4. **Evidence before Answers**
5. **From Operations to Better Decisions**

The research may merge, weaken or reject all five.

## 5. Interview design

### Target
Start with **12–18 qualitative merchant/operator interviews** across materially different operating models.

This is a discovery sample, not statistically representative market measurement.

Continue until the important job/problem patterns stabilize across relevant segments; do not claim saturation merely because the target count was reached.

### Duration
45–60 minutes.

### Interview rule
Spend most of the session on **recent real behavior**, not opinions about hypothetical software.

Ask for the last actual occurrence:
- last payout reconciliation;
- last delivery exception;
- last stock mismatch;
- last campaign/order-source question;
- last time an Order/customer had to be found across tools;
- last business decision requiring exports/calculations;
- last provider dispute or unclear status.

## 6. Core interview guide

### Operating context
1. Walk me through how an Order moves from where it starts to when you consider the money settled.
2. Which systems, spreadsheets, people and providers touch that journey?
3. Which parts do you operate yourself and which do you delegate?

### Recent-work reconstruction
4. Think of the last Order/problem where you had to look in more than one place. What happened?
5. What did you have to search, copy, ask someone for or reconstruct?
6. How long did it take and what happened because of the delay/uncertainty?

### Reconciliation
7. Show/describe the last time you checked whether operational activity and money matched.
8. What files/screens/sources did you compare?
9. What cannot you trust without checking manually?
10. What happens when the numbers do not match?

### Control
11. Tell me about the last time you needed to intervene in an Order, customer, stock, delivery, payment or team workflow.
12. What could you change directly? What required support/provider intervention?
13. Which controls would you never delegate? Which work would you happily never touch again?

### Truth/provenance
14. When a dashboard says an Order was delivered, money collected, profit made, or campaign performed, what evidence do you need before you believe it?
15. Tell me about a time a number/status looked correct but later turned out not to mean what you thought.
16. Do you care who changed something or what the previous value was? In which situations?

### Decisions
17. What recurring business decisions take the most preparation before you can decide?
18. What do you export, merge, calculate or ask someone to explain?
19. What would make you trust a recommendation enough to act on it?
20. When would you want software to recommend versus decide automatically?

### Alternatives and switching
21. What is good about your current setup that you would not want to lose?
22. What frustrates you enough that you have built a workaround?
23. Have you tried replacing any part of the setup? What happened?
24. If one problem disappeared tomorrow, which would matter most to your business?

### Language
25. How would you describe that problem to another merchant?
26. What do you call the work of checking/rebuilding/reconciling this today?

## 7. Avoid leading questions

Do not ask:
- “Would connected commercial truth be valuable?”
- “Would you like one platform for everything?”
- “Do you want more control?”
- “Would AI help you?”
- “Would you trust transparent data?”
- “Would this save you time?”

These invite agreement and contaminate positioning research.

Ask for behavior, examples, tradeoffs and existing spending/workarounds first.

## 8. Evidence capture per interview

For every material observation record:

- participant segment/context;
- role;
- countries/channels/providers used;
- concrete recent event;
- job attempted;
- current workflow/tools;
- manual work;
- frequency;
- consequence;
- workaround;
- control needed/delegated;
- evidence/provenance needed;
- decision affected;
- exact participant language;
- confidence level;
- contradiction/counterexample;
- hypothesis tags H1–H8.

Never store unnecessary personal/customer PII in the Intelligence repository.

## 9. Scoring after qualitative coding

Do not score during the interview.

After coding, assess each problem/territory across:

1. **Frequency**
2. **Severity**
3. **Economic consequence**
4. **Decision consequence**
5. **Current workaround burden**
6. **Cross-segment recurrence**
7. **Wossol current proof strength**
8. **Competitive defensibility evidence**
9. **Language resonance**
10. **Future compounding fit**

Scores summarize evidence; they do not replace quotes/examples or create statistical precision from a small qualitative sample.

## 10. Territory advancement gate

A positioning territory should advance only when all are true:

- merchants independently describe the underlying problem;
- the problem has repeated material consequence;
- current alternatives leave meaningful friction or tradeoff;
- Wossol has current Product proof for the core promise;
- claim can be expressed without relying on future Intelligence;
- competitive research does not already reduce it to generic category language;
- the territory remains portable beyond one country/provider/COD workflow;
- language can be understood without explaining Wossol's architecture first.

A territory should weaken/reject when:
- merchants rarely experience the problem;
- they prefer complete outsourcing instead;
- the problem is low consequence;
- current workaround is sufficient;
- Wossol proof is too partial;
- competitor parity makes the language generic;
- it requires unsupported future capability.

## 11. Quantitative follow-up

Only after qualitative patterns stabilize, build a survey to measure:
- prevalence;
- frequency;
- relative priority;
- segment differences;
- current tool/provider patterns;
- stated switching triggers.

Do not write the survey first and force merchants into our existing hypotheses.

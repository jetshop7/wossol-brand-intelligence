## User directions and rationale
- Strong preference for brevity and quick recall; ShopRise felt easier than TradeSpring, while most prior compounds felt too long.
- "Shop" is strategically misleading because Wossol is **not** primarily a store builder.
- User requests continuing naming promptly, not further screenshots. Brand must not lock into COD, Shop, delivery or a country.
- Recommendation: try ~4–7 Latin characters / ideally 1–3 simple syllables as a soft target, not rigid scientific law. Test natural Arabic and cross-language pronunciation.
- English familiarity is not universal in North African or Arab merchants; actual source meaning, understandable sound and recall need testing.

## Analysis
Short and meaningful is directionally apt for target buyer communication, but familiar Arabic roots in commerce/software have substantial name crowding. This screen found an especially close Algerian COD-order seller app **Zimam**; short and relevant does NOT mean ownable. Moving from Shop/Trade to other overly literal single-word merchant-management names alone will not solve collision. Avoid the false choice of globally opaque invented syllables versus heavily used generic Arabic words: explore **distinctive metaphorical real terms**, alternative semantic territories and compounds that remain genuinely short after joining, coupled with meaning/provenance and early checks.

## Recommendations (assistant, not approvals)
1. Exclude Shop as leading naming root and de-emphasize descriptive trade/shop/merchant compounds, per user feedback.
2. Screen candidates *before* presenting. Only show viable survivors with actual sound, cultural, competitive, name & trademark risk disclosures.
3. Use 3–5 candidates per externally presented shortlist, if they survive early screen; zero cleared candidates currently.
4. Keep Wossol as an unapproved baseline with known Wusool/Wosool sector overlaps.
5. Next explore less generic metaphor families, each grounded in real lexical meaning, with target-user pronunciation and spelling tests.

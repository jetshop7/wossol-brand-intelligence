# Professional Brand Naming Research and Wossol Protocol V0.1

Date: 2026-10-22
Status: UNDER_REVIEW
Scope: Naming agency methods, linguistics/consumer memory evidence, legal distinctiveness and application to Wossol. This is a methodology and research document, NOT an approved name, legal opinion or completed trademark clearance.

## Objective and context
The user asked for substantial, reputable research on how professional naming and identity agencies name companies. Previous proposals of pseudo-Romance coined names lacked audience familiarity and traceable linguistic rationale. The user prefers names that can be readily understood/remembered by Arabic-speaking merchants and remain usable across markets; real English words, transparent compounds and accessible Arabic words are candidates for exploration. Wossol/وصول remains provisional, with known name collision concerns.

## Academic sources and limits
6. Lee & Baack, 2014, Meaning or Sound? Journal of Promotion Management https://doi.org/10.1080/10496491.2014.946203 — two experiments on conceptual and perceptual fluency; relevance to recall. Specific experimental effects are not universal laws across cultures.
7. Luna, Carnevale & Lerman, 2013, Does brand spelling influence memory? Journal of Consumer Psychology https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2012.02.003 — spelling of auditory brands affects recall.
8. Smith & Abell, 2025 (2026 journal issue), The Art of Misspelling, Journal of Consumer Research https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf020 — several experiments, nuanced effects of misspelling depending on fluency and fit.
9. Brennan et al., 2024, Phonesthemes, Journal of Consumer Psychology https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.1396 — some English sound/spelling clusters can carry product-congruent associations; do not extrapolate automatically to Arabic, French, Spanish or other languages.

## Trademark authorities
10. USPTO, Strong Trademarks https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/strong-trademarks — suggestive/arbitrary/fanciful generally stronger than descriptive/generic in US trademark law; significance of pronunciation, spelling and foreign meanings.
11. INTA, Considerations in Selecting a Trademark https://www.inta.org/fact-sheets/considerations-in-selecting-a-trademark/ — spectrum of distinctiveness and tradeoffs.
12. WIPO, Global Brand Database https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database — keyword, names and jurisdictional search; national registers may also be needed.
13. WIPO, Madrid System Check Availability https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/check-availability — search exact and confusingly similar marks by sound, appearance, meaning and related services; screening does not guarantee clearance.
14. WIPO FAQ https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/faqs_branddb — database absence does not establish freedom to use and national collections may lag.

## Findings
1. Naming is downstream of positioning and competitor-name mapping; it is not a random-word exercise.
2. Names perform different tasks: suggest benefit, create a distinctive story, or identify origin; not every successful name states the product category.
3. Verbal familiarity can support recall in relevant language populations, but familiarity alone is insufficient for distinctiveness or availability.
4. Legal strength often conflicts with immediate descriptiveness. Aim for suggestive/metaphoric or meaningful yet sufficiently distinctive names rather than generic category labels.
5. Name candidates need phonetic and spelling tests in Arabic and launch languages, ideally with actual merchants.
6. The visual logo should not be used to compensate for an intrinsically hard-to-pronounce name; test name alone before visual evaluation.
7. Name screening includes identical, spelling-near, pronunciation-near and conceptually similar marks; web and domain search are first-pass only.
8. The company masterbrand must leave room for 10–15 years of expansion; category features can live in product/service descriptors and subnames.

## Recommended Wossol naming protocol (ASSISTANT RECOMMENDATION, NOT APPROVED)
Stage 0 — Decision framing: Retain Wossol as baseline candidate, not approved. Document existing Wusool/Wosool category collisions; review prior source files rather than rescraping without need.
Stage 1 — One-page Naming Brief: audience, launch markets, merchant benefit, differentiation, desired emotional impression, expansion envelope, category constraints, phonetic constraints, non-negotiables.
Stage 2 — Naming Landscape: map competitors and adjacent-sector names by semantic territory, category, syllables, languages, claimed benefits and legal collision risk.
Stage 3 — Creative routes: (A) recognizable actual-word metaphor; (B) suggestive real word; (C) transparent two-word compound with strategic tension; (D) Arabic word with cross-language writing and pronunciation. Coined names only where concept and source are defensible, not random syllables.
Stage 4 — Divergent generation: build a broad internal longlist from real semantic roots and metaphors; no premature name commitments and no invented linguistic derivations.
Stage 5 — Three sequential gates: strategic fit and candidate provenance; cross-language comprehension/pronunciation/spelling; web/category collision and preliminary trademark screen. Only survivors go to costly clearance.
Stage 6 — Behavioral audience tests: show names without logos, ask intended pronunciation; say names aloud and request unaided spelling; ask meaning/association unprompted; distract and test recall later; compare alternatives and Wossol under equivalent exposure. Record market and sample; avoid equating founder preference with audience results.
Stage 7 — Formal legal validation: jurisdictions and actual Nice goods/services, local national registers + WIPO, identical/phonetic/conceptual similarities, availability of digital handles and domains, then qualified trademark professional review before adoption.
Stage 8 — Choice and governance: evidence-weighted decision, reasons for selected and rejected names, legal uncertainty, approval by user, then verbal/visual identity development.

## Provisional decision rules
Fatal gates: severe cultural negative meaning, major credible confusingly similar commercial use in target market, irreconcilable writing/pronunciation issues for primary audience, or legal objection. Other attributes scored; do not provide fictitious precision. Prioritize merchant-comprehended meaning, pronounceability, audible spelling, category breadth, competitive differentiation, and protectability.

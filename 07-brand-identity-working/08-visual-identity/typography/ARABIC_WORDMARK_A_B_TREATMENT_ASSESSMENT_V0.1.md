## Findings and analysis
A — structural Kufi: stronger shared mass and geometry with angular C; its dense texture risks making the brand cold, rigid and less approachable. B — open humanist: more flowing Arabic joins and friendlier appearance; the contrast with C is greater, but matching all angles is not necessary for a coherent bilingual brand. Neither is custom-lettered Arabic; they are intentionally font-based prototypes, not a finished proprietary wordmark. No merchant participant, empirical legibility or delayed recall results. Not integrated in actual product dashboard. Trademark and typeface production license not cleared for final use.

## Recommendation
REFINE B as the leading Arabic typographic exploration, retain A as structural comparator. Do not approve either Arabic mark, bilingual lockup or C as final. Next build a genuinely custom Arabic refinement based on B with controlled terminal, join and optical proportions, without copying Latin angles; compare at realistic 24/32/64px and use existing merchant perception protocol.

## Decision status
Assistant desk recommendation only. No user approval of Arabic wordmark, Latin wordmark, typography, palette or icon.

## Open questions
Do Arabic-first merchants read the B form immediately? Can custom lettering improve ownability without sacrificing fluency? Does C need to soften optically to pair with B?

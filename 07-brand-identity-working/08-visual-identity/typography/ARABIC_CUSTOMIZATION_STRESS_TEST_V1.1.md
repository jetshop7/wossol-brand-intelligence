## QA observations
At display size A introduces disconnected-looking chips on waw tails and an artificial break at lam top. B compounds the issue with visual noise around sad/waw and destabilizes joining. Both create superficial geometric resemblance to Latin C but **reduce Arabic typographic integrity**. At smaller sizes cuts become fragile. The BASE remains the clearest readable Arabic treatment among these three but lacks ownable character. No native-reader testing, legal clearance, trademark study or production integration.

## Professional decision — REJECT A and B
Reject the two modifications. Do not use decorative cuts as a surrogate for professional Arabic lettering. **REFRAME** the next work unit: first define bilingual typographic architecture and operational uses (Latin-primary, Arabic-primary, compact, bilingual lockup) using high-quality type as a provisional baseline. Only then design a properly structured Arabic custom letter family with a clear typographic rationale and expert-level anatomy. If that cannot be achieved reliably in this process, retain a properly licensed Arabic font as the transparent provisional companion, not a falsely branded bespoke mark.

## Wordmark assessment

| Candidate | Construction | Readability / rhythm | Distinctiveness / personality | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Clear Stroke | Rounded monoline, open counters, custom W and linked S rhythm; developed from the R3 craft direction, not adopted as-is | Strongest immediate reading; smooth, approachable, compact | Moderate-low: friendly, but close to familiar geometric sans wordmarks; paired S has a slight collision risk at reduction | **Advance for owner review as a wordmark-only lead**, not as a selected logo |
| B — Counterform | Solid planar silhouettes, softened angular W, squarish O counters and newly constructed S bowls | Reads at presentation size; high visual weight, with S pair rhythm needing more refinement | Moderate: strongest proprietary construction of the three, but blunt/heavy and somewhat industrial | Hold as challenger; refine only if owner prefers its stronger, more assertive tone |
| C — Square Terminal | Hard-ended stroked paths and angular W, in contrast with A's rounded monoline | Legible in the large board and header pairing; remains readable as a word | Low: geometric construction feels generic and offers little ownable signature | **Reject** in this round |

The wordmark recommendation is A for owner review because clarity and warmth outweigh its current lack of signature. The existing R3 Clear Stroke is easy to read and personable, but has little ownable detail and the adjacent S forms are too alike/close to carry a signature. A keeps its open, rounded character while reworking the W and S rhythm; it still does not solve distinctiveness. This is not approval, and it does not imply that R3 itself is approved. B is a meaningful alternative rather than a spacing/weight variant; C is retained in the comparison only so its rejection is inspectable.

## Compact-mark assessment

| Concept | Relationship | 24/36/48/72px observation | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Linked SS | Shared-spine ligature derived from A's S pair | Stays visible, but the narrow 24px silhouette approaches an infinity/wave cliché and becomes visually dense | **Reject** |
| 02 — Sculpted W | Custom angular W with a central open notch, related to B's construction | Holds together across all four canvases; the notch survives, but at small sizes it still reads as a generic W initial | **Hold; strongest pairing, not distinctive enough to ship** |
| 03 — W→O continuous ligature | One continuous stroke transitions from the angular W into an open O loop; a new wordmark-derived construction, not a ring cut | Holds together on all four canvases; the W/O reading softens at 24px and may be read as generic motion/linkage | **Hold as the freshest compact candidate for owner distinctiveness review; not ready to ship** |

No concept yet clears the independent-recognition and ownability gate. W→O is the freshest derivation and deserves comparison against the existing brand-symbol history, but its motion-like read could remain generic. B/W has the clearest direct letterform match to its wordmark, though it risks a generic initial read. Recommendation: use a wordmark-first identity during further review and do not force a standalone icon. If an icon-only slot is mandatory, ask the owner to review W→O first; no additional exploration is warranted before that response.

## Pairing and use limits

The board pairs each mark with its related wordmark at a shared baseline and compact scale. A is the clearest website-header sample; the large A/B/C specimens are presentation-size construction views. These are optical desk comparisons, not cross-device or user testing. Exact-size canvases are 24×24, 36×36, 48×48 and 72×72px, with consistent placement and no surrounding frame. The designs were visually inspected after SVG-to-PNG rendering and corrected where the first render showed overlapping terminals in C.

The logo remains monochrome, exploratory, and unapproved. There is no claim of native-reader or merchant validation. Colors, Arabic custom lettering, slogans, advertising and unrelated identity elements were outside this task. Arabic research and prior work remain preserved; custom Arabic development is deferred rather than rejected or approved.

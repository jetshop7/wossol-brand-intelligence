# Two Full Brand Identity Directions — Creative Review V0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** EXPLORATORY / OWNER REVIEW REQUIRED
**Scope:** Two strategically grounded, bilingual identity systems and illustrative desktop, mobile, marketing, and compact-brand applications.
**Decision status:** No logo, icon, palette, typeface, slogan, or complete identity is approved. Wossol / وصول remains the user-selected working name; legal clearance remains pending.

## Executive conclusion

Two materially different creative systems are now expressed in editable vector artwork:

- **A — Clear Carry:** Latin-led, built around the existing custom angular R2-C Wossol wordmark and a calm editorial/operational system. Its intended character is composed, direct and practical.
- **B — Arabic-First Capability:** Arabic-led, with وصول primary and WOSSOL as its Latin companion. Its intended character is locally rooted, bilingual by architecture, and more expressive through reading order and language rather than an abstract technology symbol.

The difference is substantive in lead language, logo architecture, typographic hierarchy, color behavior, layout rhythm and voice. They are not yet complete proprietary identity systems: both compact tests rely on the same uncustomized Arabic initial, waw (و), and therefore do not solve the compact-signature or brand-recognition problem identified in earlier rounds. A is the stronger **next refinement lead**, not a selection: it has a stronger existing Latin wordmark asset and a clearer first-pass link to the user-selected international masterbrand, but its angular letterforms need a serious small-size and warmth/approachability review. B should remain a genuine alternative, especially if Arabic-market legibility, pronunciation and brand recall are given greater priority; its current Arabic treatment is a font-rendered reference, not custom lettering.

Neither direction has been tested with merchants, checked in production, cleared for type licensing or trademarked, or assessed for recognition. The work is a design exploration, not a final identity recommendation to adopt.

## Strategic foundation

The work follows the current approved strategic boundaries and hypotheses in the Integrated Brand & Creative Brief and the concept/creative-direction files:

- Wossol is intended to bridge a commercial operating partnership and a merchant-controlled platform; the design should not imply that software autonomously replaces merchant judgment.
- The long-term horizon is ambitious, but present-day claims must remain within demonstrated product capabilities. The example data and interfaces in these boards are explicitly schematic.
- Operational simplification and market access are complementary strategic themes. These directions express that through legibility and usable hierarchy, not unverified promises of growth or guaranteed outcomes.
- Wossol / وصول is the user's selected working name, but direct adjacent-market naming conflicts have been found and trademark clearance is incomplete. These visuals do not imply legal clearance.
- No user-approved positioning, tagline, logo, palette, font, icon or graphic device was supplied. Copy, color and typography below are hypotheses for comparative testing.

The creative distinction is intentionally made through wordmark architecture and information behavior, not by assigning a generic abstract “technology” icon to either route.

## Previous exploration audit and response

The relevant sequence was reviewed, not restarted: V1.3–V1.8 and the earlier bilingual/wordmark and compact-icon studies indexed in the project. The documented outcomes include rejection or non-approval of generic open-field, reference-frame, choice-grid and dual-aperture devices; rejection of the paired-O compact candidate at V1.8; repeated genericity in previous abstract symbol experiments; lack of custom, optically balanced Arabic/Latin construction; and an unresolved 36px signature requirement. The existing C/R2-C wordmark remains exploratory and unapproved.

This round responds by using two different wordmark-led architectures and by making applications, rather than another isolated symbol, the principal comparison surface. It does **not** claim that the shared waw avatar resolves the prior compact gap. Both avatar treatments are explicitly labeled tests only; no distinctive-ownership or recognition claim is made.

## Direction A — Clear Carry

**Creative idea:** Let the bilingual name do the identifying; use calm editorial hierarchy to make complex work easier to scan.

**System hypothesis:**

- **Logo architecture:** Latin-first WOSSOL from the existing R2-C angular custom wordmark, with وصول as an intact Arabic companion. The Arabic is live text from a system font in this board, not custom lettering or an optically completed lockup.
- **Compact route:** Arabic waw in a dark rounded square, tested at 72px and 36px on the board. It is provisional, not custom, and not recognition-validated.
- **Palette proposal:** dark petrol `#142B2B`, electric lime `#D9F064`, warm paper `#F5F2EA`, and muted rust `#C2614B`. Lime is an accent hypothesis, not a chosen accessibility-safe action color.
- **Typography proposal:** humanist grotesk for Latin and a readable Naskh/humanist Arabic companion. No family is selected; licensing, native-script rendering and actual size legibility remain unverified.
- **Graphic/layout behavior:** high-contrast editorial hierarchy, generous warm-paper fields, dark navigation/anchor surfaces, lime used sparingly for focus. No repeatable proprietary graphic signature has passed a distinctiveness gate.
- **Voice hypothesis:** “Commerce work, kept in context.” / “From order to what happens next.” These are test copy, not an approved promise or tagline.
- **Applications:** prospective website hero; schematic operational desktop dashboard; a bilingual promotional/social tile; and a separate mobile navigation/dashboard shell. Both light and dark surfaces appear in the application set; a formally specified logo reverse/clear-space suite has not yet been completed.

**Main risks:** the angular Latin wordmark can feel mechanical or dated, competes with the softer strategic character implied by merchant partnership, and has not been carefully redrawn or measured at production sizes. The Arabic companion is generic live type. A similar initial-only app mark remains insufficient evidence of ownability.

## Direction B — Arabic-First Capability

**Creative idea:** The partnership speaks in two voices: lead with the chosen Arabic name, and carry capability through a disciplined bilingual editorial system rather than an abstract technology icon.

**System hypothesis:**

- **Logo architecture:** وصول is primary, WOSSOL is the Latin companion. The boards compare Arabic-first desktop presentation and a right-to-left mobile shell; the two words remain intact rather than being fused into an unsupported bilingual ligature.
- **Compact route:** Arabic waw in a navy rounded square with gold detail, shown at 72px and 36px. This is the same initial concept as A in a different treatment, not a distinctive custom glyph; its small-size optical performance and recall are untested.
- **Palette proposal:** deep indigo `#1E316B`, clear blue `#B7D1FF`, warm sand `#F6F0E4`, and amber `#F2B84B`. These are comparative hypotheses; contrast and accessibility have not been verified.
- **Typography proposal:** contemporary humanist Latin sans paired with a clear contemporary Arabic sans. Neither family is selected or license-checked; the Arabic word in the board is font-rendered reference text, not custom lettering.
- **Graphic/layout behavior:** right-to-left information hierarchy is primary in Arabic applications, with intact Latin companion and localized alignment; blue carries structure, sand and amber add warmth. It relies on typographic hierarchy rather than a generic technology icon.
- **Voice hypothesis:** “Commerce operations, made clearer.” / “Your operation. Your decisions.” These are exploratory statements, not approved claims or taglines.
- **Applications:** Arabic-first prospective website hero, schematic desktop dashboard, bilingual social/promotional tile, and a separate Arabic-first RTL mobile navigation/dashboard shell.

**Main risks:** it is less differentiated at a glance unless Arabic lettering, Latin pairing and localized information behavior are developed as a bespoke coherent system. The current Arabic rendering and companion relationship are not production typography. The copy needs fluent native review, and orientation/interaction behavior requires real product design QA. The same generic initial-avatar concern applies.

## Comparative recommendation

**Continue A first, retain B as a live alternative; approve neither.** A starts from the stronger already-existing Latin wordmark source and provides a coherent low-friction international masterbrand reading. That advantage is narrow and craft-dependent: first run letterform, legibility, warmth and competitive-distinctiveness tests on the actual C paths; do not preserve C merely because it exists. B is not a derivative colorway of A: its Arabic-first lockup, RTL mobile information architecture, cultural/linguistic emphasis, and color/voice are a separate system worth preserving through the same gates.

The shared avatar is the primary unresolved system defect and weakens the claim that either is a “complete” identity. The most responsible next study is a compact-mark exploration genuinely distinct from a bare waw initial, developed alongside a proportionally refined bilingual logotype—not another abstract container/arrow/frame. Test each against prior rejected routes and a broader official visual benchmark before any merchant or owner preference can be interpreted as validation. Also obtain native Arabic editorial review and assess real interface text at production sizes.

## First-party competitive / implementation references

Official brand documentation was checked on 2026-10-25 as a focused benchmark for identity-system completeness and asset behavior, not as an exhaustive visual market audit:

1. **Shopify Brand Assets** — <https://www.shopify.com/brand-assets>. The official system describes a bag mark plus wordmark, variants and contexts for standalone-mark use, and minimum-size guidance. This supports testing primary/compact relationships and explicit size rules; it does not indicate Wossol should use an ecommerce-bag metaphor.
2. **Shopify Help: Managing brand assets** — <https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/managing-brand-assets>. This documents centrally managed brand assets and channel-specific/logo-shape requirements. It supports designing a flexible asset family and commerce-channel crops; it does not demonstrate localization limitations apply to Wossol.
3. **Odoo Brand Assets** — <https://www.odoo.com/page/brand-assets>. The official asset/guideline page supplies logo variants and usage/minimum-size direction alongside its published colors. It reinforces that a visual direction needs consistent export/use rules, not just a hero logo.
4. **Zoho Visual Guidelines** — <https://www.zoho.com/branding/visual-guidelines.html>, and its general brand resource <https://www.zoho.com/branding/>. The published system documents mark construction, color, type and usage rules. It is a useful completeness benchmark; no conclusion about direct category exclusivity or whitespace follows.
5. **Existing Wossol competitor evidence:** [Competitor Naming & Visual Landscape V0.1](../../../03-market-competitors/COMPETITOR_NAMING_VISUAL_LANDSCAPE_V0.1.md) contains selected public identity evidence for nine commerce/COD-adjacent brands; [MDM & COD Network Screenshot Visual Audit V0.1](../../../03-market-competitors/MDM_COD_NETWORK_VISUAL_SCREENSHOT_AUDIT_V0.1.md) contains supplied/observed Arabic, English, French marketing and dashboard artifacts. These are bounded samples, not a comprehensive current software-brand screenshot or trademark search.

**Research limitation:** primary-source documentation was favored, but this pass did not collect and normalize a fresh broad set of dated competitor screenshots or conduct a full identity, font, social, app-store or jurisdiction-level trademark audit. It cannot support a claim that either route occupies a unique competitive whitespace.

## Provisional usage guidance for the next round

These rules are design hypotheses pending testing—not a released brand manual:

- Keep the two language names intact. Declare one lead order per audience/surface; do not mechanically concatenate or compress Arabic and Latin to fit.
- Preserve the current prototype distinction: A is Latin-led; B is Arabic-led. Do not reduce B to A recolored or apply English left-to-right navigation assumptions to Arabic surfaces.
- Never treat the 36px waw tile as an approved logo. Test toolbar, favicon/app icon and 36px sidebar use on both light and dark grounds. If it fails, show the full wordmark where space allows and keep an explicit temporary avatar.
- Establish one approved wordmark/lockup master, optical alignment, minimum sizes, clear space, small-size redraw and light/dark variants before production exports.
- Limit any chosen palette to role tokens (text, canvas, structure, focus/accent) after contrast checks. Do not use lime/amber as small text on white without testing.
- Use actual, licensed font families with full Arabic shaping, punctuation, numerals, line-wrap and fallback testing. Require native editorial review of Arabic campaign and product copy.
- Keep marketing claims within the evidence boundary in the strategy brief; all dashboard counts, states and feature copy in these boards are fictional schematic content.
- Keep the editable SVG as source of truth; exported PNG is a review image, not a production source. Require target-size, browser, accessibility and device QA before release.

## Built
Two genuinely different **graphic grammar** routes, expressed as editable monochrome SVG:
- A / Open Field: expanding directional perimeter around an opportunity headline. This is a directional metaphor, not a symbol. Source: V13_A_OPEN_FIELD.svg.
- B / Reference Frame: four open corner rules define a merchant-centered reference field. Designed as layout grammar for headers and operational sections; not a letter or mandatory logo. Source: V13_B_REFERENCE_FRAME.svg.
- Compact scale stress: corner frame inside simulated 36, 72 and 144px slots. Source: V13_COMPACT_FRAME_TEST.svg.

Original monochrome 1440px PNG application boards were also created and inspected locally (not uploaded to GitHub). The visuals are schematic and contain sample English copy only; they are not real deployed product screenshots or official copy. Cross-language reading remains a future gate. No fabricated competitor, recognition or customer validation.

## Critique and verdict
A **REJECT** as brand language: its expanding line/arrow recalls widespread growth/path motifs and has weak uniqueness; do not recycle previous path-based experiments.
B **TEST / NOT APPROVE** as UI/editorial scaffolding: robust monochrome layout and understandable content hierarchy, with a consistent operational framing idea. However four corners are generic viewfinder/crop marks, and testing them at 36px exposes no persuasive compact brand mark. It does **not** pass a distinctiveness or independent identity gate. No proof the user recognizes WOSSOL without its lettering.
Both routes are insufficient to declare a distinctive identity. Therefore overall **REFRAME**, rather than falsely endorsing B.

## Recommendation and next unit
Do not rehash S/W or generic frame/path motifs. Develop a **strategy-led identity behavior** that is recognizable without the logo: a fixed editorial information hierarchy plus a non-generic visual relationship between expanding opportunity and merchant choice/control, tested in bilingual content and real product layout constraints. Before further geometry, compare against existing V0.4 visual grammar failures and V1.2. For genuine visual uniqueness, demand independent recognition evidence or close visual benchmark comparison. Arabic remains the correctly-shaped font-derived reference pending professional custom lettering; avoid falsely asserting bespoke type.
